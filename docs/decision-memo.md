# Scholarship foundation portfolio decision

I recommend a **90-day paper pilot** at **60% BIL / 40% VT** as the zero-additional-cost reference, with **$0 of live capital**. I ask the committee to approve the pilot and designate the portfolio analyst as its owner. Across the window and VT-return stresses, the model selected 55–85% BIL and 15–45% VT. Across the two windows and illustrative 0–100 bp cost scenarios, the range widened to 45–85% BIL, 15–55% VT, 0% IEF and 0–5% LQD. These ranges describe the weights selected in the tested portfolios. [Executed results](../outputs/week-12-result.md)

The foundation has $100 million, a seven-year horizon and quarterly scholarships of $750,000. Four payments total $3 million, which sets the 3% reserve floor. I would ask the committee whether the reserve may include IEF, whose principal fluctuates, or should use Treasury bills through BIL. The two reference-window allocations satisfy either definition. The policy comparator is Meridian's 60% global equity / 40% investment-grade bond mix. I use equal weight and inverse volatility as transparent model baselines. [Mandate](../PROJECT2-MANDATE.md)

## Evidence for the decision

The frozen October 20, 2026 UTC snapshot contains adjusted monthly closes through September 2026. At zero additional cost, the constrained search used all 59 monthly returns and chose 60% BIL / 40% VT at 6.61% estimated annual return and 6.05% volatility. The last 36 returns yielded 85% BIL / 15% VT at 6.75% return and 1.79% volatility. The 25-percentage-point weight change exceeds my five-point review limit. Cutting the full-sample VT return estimate by one percentage point moved the solution to 55% BIL / 45% VT at 6.53% return and 6.79% volatility. With BIL as the lower-risk asset, a weaker equity return estimate required more equity to clear the same hurdle. [Scenario allocations](../outputs/allocations.csv)

The full-sample equal-weight and inverse-volatility baselines estimated 3.01% and 3.37%, respectively, and both missed 6.50%. In the recent window they estimated 8.13% and 4.61%, respectively. Equal weight then passed but had 5.88% estimated volatility versus 1.79% for the constrained mix. All three passed the assumed reserve rule in both windows. The 6.50% annual screen uses 2.50% assumed inflation plus 4%. The foundation's actual objective is a rolling five-year net return above CPI by four percentage points. Evidence of meeting it would require rolling compounded returns, inflation and costs. [Baseline comparisons](../outputs/portfolio-comparison-validation.txt)

At 15 bp of additional annual cost, the unchanged 60/40 estimate falls from 6.6145% to 6.4645% and misses the screen. Reoptimization at that cost moves five points from BIL into VT, raising estimated annual volatility to 6.7901% and producing a 6.8342% after-cost estimate. The new mix clears the hurdle by taking more equity risk. At 100 bp the full-sample mix reaches 55% VT and 8.27% volatility. The small starting margin and greater risk required at higher fees support keeping this exercise on paper. ## Pilot monitoring and reversal

The portfolio analyst will record hypothetical weights, turnover, estimated costs, reserve coverage and performance versus the benchmark and two baselines at each of the three month-ends. The analyst will rerun the two windows, the one-point VT-return stress and the agreed cost/eligibility cases, then bring the record to the committee at day 90. I would use GS10 to monitor rate exposure for IEF and LQD with dated ALFRED evidence. At the September 30 cutoff, the latest visible value was August's 4.68%. September's 4.99% appears in the October 1 vintage. [GS10 validation](../outputs/gs10-validation.txt)

I would suspend the pilot and return immediately to the committee if no allocation satisfies both the agreed reserve rule and the 6.50% after-cost screen. A maximum shift above five percentage points under the designated stresses keeps live funding deferred. I would revisit live allocation only after the committee confirms reserve eligibility, verifies liquidity and implementation terms, agrees a documented cost allowance, and sees forward evidence consistent with the rolling five-year net objective and no greater than a five-point stress shift. The committee would then vote on any funded allocation. [Validation and AI-use record](validation-and-ai-use.md)
