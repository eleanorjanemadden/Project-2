Candidate D invests 100% in the Treasury 10-year note and 0% in all other holdings. 

My judgment: The objection holds because a 10-year Treasury can lose market value when interest rates rise, even though it can be sold quickly. S9 is the deciding statement because passing the liquidity checks does not establish that the portfolio will avoid a calendar-year loss greater than 10%.

Skeptical colleague review: I would cite S9 against this portfolio because Treasury securities still have interest-rate risk. I would also ask whether this allocation supports S2’s CPI + 4% objective, since the checker does not evaluate expected returns.

My Question for the CIO: What should the pilot optimize, the likelihood of meeting the CPI + 4% objective or limiting downside risk?

If the CIO prioritizes meeting the return objective, I would consider a larger allocation to diversified equities within the constraints. If downside protection is the priority, I would consider more cash and short-term government securities and discuss whether the return objective remains realistic.


Investment Policy Statement: An investment policy statement documents a client’s investment objectives, constraints, and guidelines. It gives the client and investment manager a shared basis for making and evaluating portfolio decisions. The objectives explain what the portfolio should achieve, while the constraints limit how it can be invested. Some requirements can be checked directly using holdings, while others require performance data, trading records, or clarification from the client.

Reflection

Why can a portfolio pass every checkable rule and still be wrong for this client?

The checker evaluates specific features of the holdings, but it does not establish whether the portfolio can meet the client’s return objective or risk expectations. Candidate D shows this distinction: it passes every implemented check but concentrates the portfolio in interest-rate risk. Suitability requires considering the client’s objectives and unresolved instructions together.

Which S1–S10 type was I least sure of?

I was least sure about S9 because it sounds like a hard constraint, but the statement does not define the loss measure or breach response. I classified it as an unverifiable promise as written because the checker cannot guarantee that future calendar-year losses will stay below 10%.
